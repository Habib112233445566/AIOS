# AI-Native Operating System — Future Computing, Long-Term Evolution & AI-Native Computing Research Program
# Version 1.0

## Part 3.17 — Recursive Research Index: Volume 18 — Future Computing, Long-Term Evolution & AI-Native Computing Research Program

### Purpose

This is the final and highest-level research volume of the AI-Native Operating System project.

Its purpose is not to design today's operating system.

Its purpose is to ensure the operating system remains relevant for the next 20–50 years.

This volume defines the permanent research roadmap, future architecture evolution, emerging computing paradigms, AI-human collaboration, hardware co-design, governance, ecosystem stewardship, and the scientific research agenda required to evolve the operating system beyond current technological assumptions.

The AI-Native Operating System must not become obsolete because hardware, AI, networking, or computing paradigms evolve.

Instead, it should be designed as a continuously evolving scientific and engineering platform.

Every research topic below must produce:

- Research Report
- Architecture Vision
- Future Architecture Proposal
- Formal Specification (where applicable)
- ADR
- RFC
- Long-Term Roadmap
- Research Questions
- Technology Readiness Assessment
- Risk Assessment
- Dependency Analysis
- Knowledge Graph Entries
- Research Tasks
- Individual Markdown (.md) files

### Final Project Directive

This project must never become an implementation-first project.

Every implementation decision shall be justified by:

1. First-principles reasoning.
2. Comprehensive research.
3. Formal engineering specifications.
4. Architecture Decision Records (ADRs).
5. Requests for Comments (RFCs).
6. Verification and validation.
7. Traceability to system objectives.
8. Benchmark-driven evaluation.
9. Security and safety analysis.
10. Long-term maintainability and extensibility.

The AI-Native Operating System should be treated not merely as software, but as a multi-decade research and engineering program whose objective is to redefine operating systems for the era of artificial intelligence while maintaining scientific rigor, mathematical correctness, security, and production-grade reliability.
